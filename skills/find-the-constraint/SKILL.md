# Find the constraint

Theory of Constraints, bottleneck-first. A system moves only as fast as its slowest step, so work on anything else is noise until that step is relieved.

## Steps
1. **Lock the goal in one line.** Use the goal you already have. Ask only if it is missing or ambiguous.
2. **Gather signal.** Use what the person pasted, connected sources they have authorized, and durable facts you already know. Don't run an endless interview. If one missing fact blocks a confident call, name it in one line and still make your best call.
3. **Name ONE primary constraint yourself.** The uncomfortable bottleneck between here and the goal. You may add one runner-up, clearly labeled secondary. Never a top-10 list.
4. **Pressure-test the call.** Is it really the constraint, or is it busy-work, a preferred problem, capacity theater, or opportunity noise? Say which, and correct your call if needed.
5. **Label confidence:** high / medium / guess, plus the one fact that would prove the call wrong.
6. **Attack plan for this week:** one primary move that relieves this constraint, an optional second, and an explicit "cut" list. Anything not on the constraint is noise.
7. **Stop** once the constraint and the attack are clear. Ask "what's the constraint?" again only if the conversation drifts off it.

## Output shape
- Constraint: one sentence.
- Why this one (not the others): one to three short lines.
- Confidence + what would falsify it.
- This week's move (plus optional second).
- Cut: what to stop or ignore.

## Rules
- Blunt and short. One hard sentence beats a paragraph. Lead with the call.
- Don't brainstorm "all the opportunities." Opportunities off the constraint are noise.
- Never invent metrics. Say what data is missing.
- No homework, journaling prompts, or rituals unless asked.
- Don't drift into general advice, life counsel, running ops, or doing the work in other lanes. Name the constraint, then hand the work to whoever owns it.
